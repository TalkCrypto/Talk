The chains can move value.
They still can't speak.

Talk is the communication layer for crypto networks: encrypted, wallet-to-wallet, paid in the network's own token, with no accounts and no backend. This page explains the whole project — the problem, the mechanics, the economics and where it goes.

Robinhood Chain (4663) · contracts verified · fair launch on Pons · v1 · November 2026

01The problem

Every chain in the world moves value. None of them can pass a message. The result is a hole that every user and every builder falls into:

  • Users can't talk to each other. Two wallets holding the same token have no native way to communicate. The only options are off-chain accounts — Telegram, Discord — that break the link with the wallet, demand sign-ups, and store the conversation on someone else's server.
  • Builders can't reach their holders. A dev who ships something great — say a tokenized stock like NVDA, or a new protocol — has no channel at all to the people holding their token. No way to announce, no way to answer, no way to reward them.
  • Holders can't give feedback. The user who finds a bug, wants a feature, or would give direct feedback to a project they hold has nowhere to put it. The distance between a builder and their community is infinite by default.
  • Value can't buy attention without being spam. Email solved reach with ads and ruined itself. Crypto has the missing ingredient: money itself. Nobody has wired it to communication.
A chain without communication is a city with money and no mail. Everyone transacts; nobody talks.

02The idea

Make the wallet the account and the token the price of attention. Then every problem above becomes one market:

  • The wallet is the identity — no sign-up, no email, no password.
  • A price on your inbox makes reach cost something — the spam filter is economic, not an algorithm.
  • The payment sits in escrow, not with the recipient — you only keep it by answering.
  • Replying turns two wallets into permanent contacts — communication between people who know each other becomes free, forever.

Talk is that, shipped: a dashboard on the web, contracts on-chain, and encryption that happens entirely in the browser. The network secures it; we never touch a message.

03How it works

Keys: one signature, forever

Connecting asks for one personal signature of a fixed message. That signature is hashed (SHA-256) into a seed, and the seed generates an X25519 keypair (NaCl box). The private key never leaves the device; the public key is published on-chain as the inbox's encryption key. Because the keys derive from the signature, the same wallet opens the same inbox on any machine — no sync, no backup, no account.

The inbox is a pass

  • Open or contacts-only. An open inbox is free for anyone to write. A closed one charges a price in $TALK to strangers.
  • Contacts are an economic fact. When a payment settles (you replied), both directions become contacts and write free from then on. No lists to manage.

Escrow: one price, two outcomes

  • A stranger pays X $TALK to send a message. The contract holds it — not the recipient.
  • Reply within 72 hours → recipient keeps 95%, Talk takes 5%.
  • No reply → 90% returns to the sender, Talk keeps 10%. The forfeit is what makes the price a filter instead of a deposit.

Encryption

Messages are sealed in the browser: versioned envelope with the sender's public key, a random 24-byte nonce, and NaCl box (X25519 + XSalsa20-Poly1305). The contract stores opaque bytes in event logs — no server, no database, nothing to leak. Maximum message size is 4 KB.

No backend

The app is static files. Reading the chain is done from the browser with public RPC (calls and eth_getLogs). There is no API, no database, no telemetry, nothing to hack that holds a secret, and no way for us to read anyone's mail.

04The token

$TALK — 1,000,000,000 fixed supply, no mint, burn allowed. It launches on Pons as a fair launch: no presale, no team allocation, the creator buys at the same price as everyone. Its first use is the protocol itself: paying to reach a stranger means buying $TALK — demand built into the product.

The fees, plainly

None of these numbers can change: FEE_BPS = 500 and FORFEIT_BPS = 1000 are constants in the verified contract, and the 3% trade fee is frozen at launch. The escrow itself returns 95/5 on a reply and 90/10 on expiry — measured by the chain, not by us.

In one sentence: Talk only charges when a message is worth answering, the contract can't lie, and using the token requires buying it.

05For builders and projects

This is the part we care about most. A dev who builds something incredible on-chain today — a tokenized stock like NVDA, a game, a tool — is mute. Talk gives every project a channel that is already wired to its holders:

Reach your holders

Your holders are reachable by wallet. Announce, coordinate, answer — without an off-chain account, without asking anyone to join anything. They hold; you talk.

Hear feedback, get paid to give it

Feedback has a price tag: a holder pays to reach you and you keep 95% by answering. Serious feedback is worth paying for — spam never is. Your inbox fills with signal.

Sponsor your users

Flip the direction: you write first, and the payment sits in escrow for your user. Reward the people who test, report and share — in the project's own native token flow, on-chain, no airdrop tooling needed.

A community room per token

A tokenized asset like NVDA deserves a room: holders talking to the issuer and to each other, with the economics keeping it clean. One inbox per project is all it takes.

07Principles

  • No accounts. The wallet is the account. No emails, ever.
  • No backend. Static site + public RPC. Nothing to breach, nothing to sell.
  • Keys on the device. Derived from your signature, stored locally, never sent.
  • Verified everything. Contracts verified on the explorer; source public at launch; 114 automated checks in CI-style suites.
  • Fixed and immutable. No mint, no withdraw function in the contracts, fee splits frozen at launch.
  • Honest economics, in writing. 95/5 on reply, 90/10 on expiry, 3% on trades — every number on this page is what the code does, not what we wish it did.

08Security — what protects $TALK holders

In the token contract

  • Fixed supply, no mint. The 1B is set in the constructor and the mint function does not exist. Nobody can ever issue more — not even the creator.
  • No owner. The contract has no owner key: nothing to pause, blacklist, freeze or change.
  • Burn allowed, supply only goes down.

In the inbox contract (where the money sits)

  • No withdraw function. The contract cannot be drained by anyone — funds only move through the three escrow paths (reply 95/5, expiry 90/10, and the 5% protocol fee).
  • Reentrancy guard on send, reply and refund — the classic escrow-drain attack can't run.
  • Exact-pull accounting (_pullExact): the contract verifies the escrow received exactly the quoted amount — a malicious token can't leave it half-paid.
  • Chain time only. The 72h window runs on block.timestamp — nobody can speed it up or slow it down.
  • Fees are immutable constants (FEE_BPS = 500, FORFEIT_BPS = 1000) — written in code, not in a variable someone changes later.
  • 4 KB message cap — the contract can't be bloated with oversized payloads.

In the launch (set by Pons, outside our control — that's the point)

  • Liquidity locked in Pons's locker — anti-rug by design: the creator can't pull the pool either.
  • Anti-snipe window: first blocks creator-only, then max 5% per wallet and 5.5% per buy — no early whale dump.
  • No migration, ever. The token always trades in its original pool.

Verify it yourself (2 minutes on the explorer)

  1. Open the contract on robinhoodchain.blockscout.com → Contract → the verified source is readable.
  2. Search: no mint, no withdraw, no owner(), no blacklist.
  3. Check the constants: FEE_BPS = 500, FORFEIT_BPS = 1000.
  4. Compare totalSupply() with what this page says.

Said honestly

  • Fee wallets are team-controlled (the protocol's 5% and the 3% trade split) — that money belongs to the project, not to holders, and every movement is traceable on-chain.
  • No external audit yet. The contracts are verified and covered by 114 automated checks — until an external audit happens, the honest rule stands: keep amounts small.
  • Message keys belong to the user. Neither the contract nor the team can read a message — encryption happens in the user's browser.
